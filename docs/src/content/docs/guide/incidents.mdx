---
title: Incidents
description: Track exploited vulnerabilities, leaked secrets and malicious packages from signal to closure, with CRA Article 14 clocks and proof of client notification
---

An incident binds a signal to the versions and clients it affects, derives the actions
it requires, keeps the regulatory clocks, delivers documents to clients with a
verifiable record, and closes itself when no action remains. At any moment an incident
page says either *these actions remain, due by these times*, or *closed, here is why*.

Hibou never decides for you. A person decides whether an exploited vulnerability is
reportable, meaning owed to the CSIRT and ENISA under Article 14 (clients and the
component's maintainer are separate duties, see the
[CRA guide](/guide/cra-compliance/#third-party-components-the-ladder)), and when the
organization became aware of it (T0). Hibou derives what
follows, counts the clocks, records every step on the incident's timeline, and refuses
to close the incident while anything is pending.

## Kinds

| Kind | Opened by | What it tracks |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Exploited vulnerability | a known-exploited catalogue entry (CISA KEV, ENISA EUVD) on a released version, or a person | per-release VEX statements, filings, client notices, the fix |
| Security incident (CRA Art. 14(3)) | a person: build chain or signing key compromised, update server breached | filings, client notices, remediation |
| Secret leak | a secret finding | revocation, rotation, exposure assessment, removal |
| Malicious package | a malware finding | removal, CI secret rotation, registry report, client notices if it shipped |
| Policy breach | a finding crossing your own line (EPSS, severity, CWE) | a fix or an approved exception, by an internal deadline |

## Rules

Rules decide which findings open incidents. They are filter expressions, the same
grammar as the search bar, in the repository's `.hibou.yaml`:

```yaml
version: 1

incidents:
  owner: team:product-security          # who receives signals and countdowns
  rules:                                # first matching rule wins
    - match: "exploited:true"           # refs: released (default)
      regimes: [cra-article-14]
      priority: P1
    - match: "type:secret verified:true"  # refs: all (default for secrets and malware)
      priority: P1
    - match: "type:malware"
      priority: P1
    - match: "epss:>=0.5 OR (severity:critical AND fix:available)"
      refs: [released, default]
      due: 7d
      priority: P2
```

| Key | Default | Meaning |
|-----|---------|---------|
| `match` | required | A filter expression. `exploited:true`, `epss:>=10%`, `cvss:>=9`, `fix:available`, `verified:true`, `type:secret` all work. |
| `kind` | derived | Secrets open secret leaks, malware opens malicious packages, an exploited dependency matched by a rule asking for exploitation opens an exploited vulnerability, anything else a policy breach. |
| `refs` | by kind | `released` (snapshots pinned by a release), `default` (the default branch), `all` (every branch and pull request), or branch globs such as `release/*`. Secrets and malware default to `all`, the rest to `released`. |
| `regimes` | `[internal]` | `cra-article-14`, `nis2-article-23`, `national:<code>`, `internal`. |
| `priority` | `P2` | Your own triage order. |
| `due` | none | An internal deadline for a policy breach, e.g. `7d` or `48h`. |

The three first rules above are the built-in defaults: a repository that writes nothing
still opens incidents on exploited, verified-secret and malware findings. An
organization can replace the defaults and lock rules repositories cannot remove, in
**Settings → Incidents**.

## Finding incidents

Each repository has an **Incidents** tab with its own incidents; the organization's
**Incidents** tab lists them all. In the vulnerability list, `incident:true` keeps the
findings with a linked incident (opened on the finding, or on its advisory in that
repository, in any state) and `incident:false` the others. A finding's detail shows its
incidents as a badge in the header.

## Merging duplicates

**Merge into…** folds a duplicate into the incident that stays: its timeline, notices
and completed actions move over, its pending actions are dropped, and the survivor is
recomputed. Pick the survivor from the list; only incidents of the same kind on the same
repository can merge, and an exploited vulnerability only with one about the same CVEs.
With four-eyes on, one person proposes the merge and another applies it. A merge cannot
be undone. Hibou merges on its own only when two advisories name the same vulnerability,
such as a CVE and its GHSA.

## The decision and the clocks

For an exploited vulnerability in a third-party component, the decision follows the
ladder the Commission's guidance on the CRA draws (C(2026) 5252, §218): the first step
that applies, in order. Each step is also the VEX statement it means for the releases in
scope.

| | Step | Reportable? | VEX |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No shipped version integrates the vulnerable version | No; nothing else owed | `not_affected` · `component_not_present` |
| 2 | Present, but it cannot be exploited in our product (the vulnerable code never runs, its input is not attacker-controlled, a mitigation blocks it) | No; report it to the component maintainer unless they already know | `not_affected` · the justification |
| 3 | Exploitable in our product, no reliable evidence it was exploited here | No; fix or mitigate without delay, warn the clients that run it if useful | `affected` |
| 4 | Reliable evidence it was exploited in our product | **Yes**: T0, early warning within 24 h, notification within 72 h, final report 14 days after a fix is available; impacted users informed | `affected` |

A known-exploited catalogue entry (CISA KEV, ENISA EUVD) is the signal that opens the
incident. It says the vulnerability is exploited *somewhere*; steps 1 to 4 say whether
it is in your product, exploitable there, and exploited there.

The decision is one per incident: it answers for the product. Recording it writes the
matching VEX statement for every release in scope that has none yet (untick the box to
skip that); a release that differs gets its own statement in **Scope**, and that
statement takes precedence. **T0** is when, after the initial assessment, you had a
reasonable degree of certainty that the vulnerability in your product is being exploited
(guidance §213); it moves backwards only, and never after a filing.

Filings are made by a person on the single reporting platform; each filing action
links to the portal (**Open portal**), Hibou pre-fills the content (**Filing draft**)
and records the reference you got back. A national regime can carry its own portal URL.
Actions are listed in the order the work happens: early warning, notification, VEX,
client notices, fix and other remediation, upstream notice, final report. An organization can
set stricter internal targets; the regulatory deadline stays shown next to them.

With **four-eyes** on, a decision that ends obligations (not reportable, dismissed) and
every waiver are proposed by one person and approved by another.

For the regulation in plain words, see [CRA compliance](/guide/cra-compliance/).

## Scope and VEX

The scope of an exploited vulnerability is every release whose inventory carries the
advisory, joined to the clients actively running it. Each release ends with a VEX
statement or an explicit exclusion with a reason. A release with clients but no
inventory is **unknown** and holds the incident open until an inventory is produced or
a statement assumes a status. A row's branch or version links to the findings behind it: straight to the finding for an incident opened on one (policy breach, secret, malicious package), and to the list filtered with `incident:<number>` for an advisory, which may match several components on one branch.

Statements are never edited: a change supersedes the previous one, and the history keeps
both. Triage writes them too: marking a dependency finding *not affected* names the VEX
justification.

For a vulnerability in your own code, record a **first-party advisory** in the
repository's **Settings → Incidents & product** (identifier, affected range in `vers`
syntax). Set the product identity in `.hibou.yaml`:

```yaml
product:
  name: Example Product
  purl: pkg:generic/example/product
  cpe: cpe:2.3:a:example:product:*:*:*:*:*:*:*:*
```

## Client notices

**Send notice** renders, per client, a CSAF security advisory scoped to the versions the
client runs, OpenVEX and CycloneDX statements, and a readable notice. Channels, set per
client under **Clients → Security notices**: email (through the organization's email
integration, to the client's security contacts), webhook (signed), portal (a token bound
to the client), or manual (you send it and record how).

Every document is stored under its SHA-256, and every delivery writes an audit entry with
the digests. **Verify** re-renders each document from its recorded inputs and compares
byte for byte. Hibou proves that a document was handed to a channel at a time; it cannot
prove it was read. Acknowledgements (link, portal download, webhook 2xx) are recorded
when they happen, and can be required in Settings.

The **dossier** bundles the timeline, scope, actions, clocks, deliveries and documents
with a SHA-256 manifest, for an authority or a client in a dispute. The dossier is not signed.

## Reference and autolinks

An incident carries one optional **reference**: the ticket or link your team tracks it
under, edited in the header (`ref`). It replaces the per-action reference; completing
an action takes a note only, and filings keep the platform's submission reference.

In **Settings → General → Autolink references**, an organization declares its tracker
prefixes the way a forge does: a prefix (`SEC-`), a target URL with `<num>`, and whether
letters may follow the prefix. From then on `SEC-7` is a link wherever people write:
incident notes, decision and waiver reasons, the reference, finding acceptance reasons
and comments. Bare `https://` URLs are linked too.

## Trackers

Add a Jira, GitHub issues, GitLab issues or webhook integration in **Settings →
Integrations** to mirror each incident as an issue: the timeline as comments, state and
priority as labels. Hibou stays the source of truth. Inbound, a mapped tracker status can
complete a manual action, and a comment starting with `hibou:` becomes a note; nothing
from the tracker can set T0, change the scope, waive an action or close an incident.

## CLI

```sh
hibou incident list
hibou incident status 12
hibou incident decide 12 --state reportable --step attacked --reason "customer evidence" --action "upgrade to 4.2.2"
hibou incident decide 13 --state not_reportable --step not_reachable --justification inline_mitigations_already_exist --reason "entities disabled"
hibou incident filing-draft 12 --step early_warning
hibou incident action 12 <action-id> file --reference SRP-2026-0042
hibou incident deliver 12 --client acme --measures "upgrade to 4.2.2"
hibou incident export 12 -o incident-12.zip
hibou verify-document 12 <delivery-id>
```
